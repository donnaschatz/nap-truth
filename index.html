Palmon Survival

A strategic multiplayer game about alliances, survival, and the NAP protocol.


What is NAP?
Non-Aggression Pact: A system that claims to bring peace, but actually centralizes power and resources.

Who Benefits?
Top alliances and spenders. Most players become assets, not partners.

End Result?
Server stagnation, mass migration, and collapse.

I. Functional Definition

A "NAP 20" (the number being variable) is presented to the general server population as a treaty for peace and stability. This is a deliberate misrepresentation.

In practice, the NAP is a Resource & Power Cartel. It is an extra-governmental body formed by the server's top-spending alliances to achieve three primary objectives:

  • Risk Elimination: To insulate the significant real-money investments of their top members from the inherent risks of organic, peer-to-peer conflict.
  • Market Control: To monopolize the server's most valuable resources, territories, and event rewards, creating a closed economic loop that benefits only its members.
  • Competitive Suppression: To systematically halt the progression of any non-member alliance that demonstrates the potential to challenge their dominance, thereby cementing a permanent power hierarchy.
The NAP does not prevent war. It nationalizes it. The NAP council becomes the only body with the legitimate authority to sanction violence, which it then uses to enforce its own economic and social policies.

Who are you? The “protected” underclass. Your alliance leader made a deal: you’re now an academy, sub-farm, or official ally. You survive, but at a cost.

III. Historical Occurrences: Case Studies

All sections below are expandable! Click the arrow or section title to reveal more details.

Server: KVK 1093 (2021)
Impact: Months of strict NAP enforcement led to a monopoly on rewards and resources by the top alliances. When cross-server war (KvK) arrived, most players were unskilled in mass PvP. The result: humiliating defeat, mass exodus, and a server left with only the NAP council and their farm accounts.
Key Insight: Discord logs show leaders negotiating event outcomes weeks in advance. Lower alliances were forced into subservience or migration. NAP breeds stagnation, not strength.
Server: Kingdom 432 (2020)
Impact: Alliances formed a rotating NAP, dictating event winners. Rewards were distributed by council vote, not competition. The server became a PvE farming simulator, with only KvK providing real conflict.
Key Insight: Over 60% of mid-core players migrated to younger kingdoms, leaving the server top-heavy and stagnant. Scripted peace kills engagement.
Server: 570 (2022)
Impact: NAP council unified the server for SvS, outlawing all internal wars. Violators were zeroed. Resource and territory control was managed via Discord spreadsheets.
Key Insight: The server lost all strategic depth and required a merger to survive. Players described it as "living in a digital bureaucracy." NAP turns games into chores.
Server: Kingdom 101 (2019)
Impact: NAP between top 10 houses led to scripted events and no organic conflict. All wars were sanctioned; unsanctioned aggression met with mass retaliation.
Key Insight: Global chat died, and only diplomats and resource managers remained active. By the next server merge, only 3 of the original top 10 houses survived. NAP suffocates community.
Server: State 53 (2023)
Impact: NAP enforced by Discord council with detailed rules. Ambitious F2P players left for newer states, and the server became a closed ecosystem.
Key Insight: When forced open PvP arrived, the NAP council couldn’t rally enough fighters, resulting in catastrophic loss and further decline. NAP weakens competitive edge.
Server: S28 (2023)
Impact: Top three alliances monopolized Behemoth Lairs, creating strict rotations and suppressing smaller alliances. When cross-server season began, the general player base was inexperienced and outmaneuvered.
Key Insight: Defeat and migration followed, as players tired of the "scripted" server. NAP stifles growth and skill.
Server: World 112 (2022)
Impact: Powerful alliances colluded to pre-determine the governor and divide central zones before the first war. Non-coalition alliances became farm accounts.
Key Insight: By cross-world events, the server was filled with high-power players who had never truly fought, resulting in poor coordination and consistent losses. NAP kills competition before it starts.
Server: City Server 2045 (2023)
Impact: Top 5 alliances monopolized high-level Raven units and bunkers, locking out the rest. Resource monopoly led to massive power imbalance.
Key Insight: During the first server-wide event, under-equipped players were steamrolled. The defeat demoralized the population and solidified the server’s reputation as a "whale’s paradise." NAP creates unbridgeable divides.
Server: Server 66 (2021)
Impact: NAP maximized SvS performance, assigning cities and resources by spreadsheet. Internal conflict was outlawed; violators shamed and zeroed.
Key Insight: The game became a bi-weekly cycle of prepping for SvS, with no room for politics or strategy. Many left for younger servers with real gameplay. NAP turns games into waiting rooms.
System: Global (Ongoing)
Impact: The Farm War Alliance (FWA) manipulates matchmaking for easy loot, circumventing competition. Players attack with minimal troops to "lose," while the designated winner gets maximum stars.
Key Insight: While efficient for farming, it atrophies attacking skills, leaving players ineffective in real wars. NAP-style systems erode mastery.

IV. The Cycle of Stagnation

This leads to the predictable Cycle of Stagnation:

  1. Phase 1 - Chaos: Server launch. Organic warfare. High activity.
  2. Phase 2 - Consolidation: Whales tire of risk. The NAP is formed.
  3. Phase 3 - Order: The NAP solidifies control. The pyramid structure is cemented.
  4. Phase 4 - Apathy: The lack of meaningful conflict leads to boredom. Player activity drops. The server's overall skill level decays.
  5. Phase 5 - Collapse: The server either dies a slow death from inactivity or is mercy-killed by a more aggressive, active server in a server-wide event, triggering a final exodus.

A NAP does not preserve a server. It embalms it.

V. Server Health Diagnosis: Symptoms of NAP Disease

Is your server infected? The symptoms are clinical and easily observable.

  • [ ] A Dead Global Chat: The primary sign of a sedated player base. When there is no conflict, there is nothing to talk about.
  • [ ] Scripted Events: Top alliances "take turns" winning events or agree on reward distribution beforehand. There is no real competition.
  • [ ] "No Kill" Rules: The NAP enforces complex rules about who can hit whom, when, and where. Hitting resource tiles may be forbidden. Attacking cities below a certain power level may be a "war crime." This is not peace; it is micromanagement.
  • [ ] Mass Migration of Mid-Core Players: The most ambitious F2P and low-spenders start leaving the server for younger, more chaotic kingdoms.
  • [ ] Rise of the "Diplomat" Player: The most influential figures on the server are not the best warriors, but the players who spend all day in Discord negotiating and managing the delicate NAP politics.
  • [ ] The Illusion of Choice: You are "free" to do anything, as long as it does not challenge the established order in any meaningful way.

If you can check three or more of these boxes, your server is in the advanced stages of NAP-induced decay.

What You Can Do

  • Unite with other independents: Form coalitions to challenge the status quo.
  • Promote open competition: Encourage events and activities that reward skill, not just diplomacy.
  • Educate new players: Share this analysis and help others recognize the signs of NAP decay.
  • Push for transparency: Demand open council meetings and fair event rules.
  • Consider migration: If the server is beyond saving, move to a younger, more active server.
  • Stay vigilant: The cycle repeats. Knowledge is your best weapon.
