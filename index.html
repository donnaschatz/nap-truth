A strategic multiplayer game about alliances, survival, and the NAP protocol.


What is NAP?
Non-Aggression Pact: A system that claims to bring peace, but actually centralizes power and resources.

Who Benefits?
Top alliances and spenders. Most players become assets, not partners.

End Result?
Server stagnation, mass migration, and collapse.

I. Functional Definition

A "NAP 20" (the number being variable) is presented to the general server population as a treaty for peace and stability. This is a deliberate misrepresentation.

In practice, the NAP is a Resource & Power Cartel. It is an extra-governmental body formed by the server's top-spending alliances to achieve three primary objectives:

  • Risk Elimination: To insulate the significant real-money investments of their top members from the inherent risks of organic, peer-to-peer conflict.
  • Market Control: To monopolize the server's most valuable resources, territories, and event rewards, creating a closed economic loop that benefits only its members.
  • Competitive Suppression: To systematically halt the progression of any non-member alliance that demonstrates the potential to challenge their dominance, thereby cementing a permanent power hierarchy.
The NAP does not prevent war. It nationalizes it. The NAP council becomes the only body with the legitimate authority to sanction violence, which it then uses to enforce its own economic and social policies.

III. Historical Occurrences: Case Studies

The following real-world examples document the impact of NAP protocols across major games. Click to expand each case:

Server: KVK 1093 (2021)
Summary: After months of strict NAP enforcement, the server's top alliances monopolized all major rewards and resources. When the first cross-server war (KvK) arrived, the majority of players were unskilled in mass PvP. The result: a humiliating defeat, mass player exodus, and a server left with only the original NAP council and their farm accounts. Discord logs from the period show top leaders negotiating event outcomes weeks in advance, with lower alliances forced into subservience or migration.
Server: Kingdom 432 (2020)
Summary: Alliances formed a coalition with a rotating NAP, dictating which guilds could win Guildfest and other events. Internal chat leaks revealed that event rewards were distributed by council vote, not competition. The server became a PvE farming simulator, with only KvK providing real conflict. By mid-year, over 60% of mid-core players had migrated to younger kingdoms, leaving the server top-heavy and stagnant.
Server: 570 (2022)
Summary: The NAP council unified the server for SvS, outlawing all internal wars. Any violation was punished by coordinated zeroing. Resource and territory control was managed via spreadsheets shared in Discord. The server lost all strategic depth, and by the end of the year, required a merger to survive. Player interviews described the experience as "living in a digital bureaucracy."
Server: Kingdom 101 (2019)
Summary: A NAP between the top 10 houses led to scripted events and a total lack of organic conflict. The NAP council sanctioned all wars, and any unsanctioned aggression was met with mass retaliation. The server's global chat died, and the only active players were diplomats and resource managers. By the next server merge, only 3 of the original top 10 houses remained active.
Server: State 53 (2023)
Summary: The NAP protocol was enforced by a Discord council, with detailed rules about tile hits, city attacks, and event participation. The most ambitious F2P players left for newer states, and the server became a closed ecosystem. When a major event forced open PvP, the NAP council was unable to rally enough active fighters, resulting in a catastrophic loss and further population decline.
Server: S28 (2023)
Summary: The server's top three alliances formed a pact to control all Behemoth Lairs. They created a strict rotation for captures, ensuring they monopolized the powerful artifacts and server-wide buffs. Any smaller alliance that attempted a capture was immediately targeted and suppressed. When the first cross-server season (Dragon Trail) began, the server's general player base was inexperienced in coordinating large-scale Behemoth raids. They were consistently outmaneuvered by enemy servers that had fostered competitive environments, leading to a crushing defeat and a wave of migrations from players tired of the "scripted" server.
Server: World 112 (2022)
Summary: Before the first Fortress War could even begin, the most powerful alliances colluded in Discord to pre-determine the server's governor and divide the map's four central zones. This "Season 0 Handshake" agreement effectively ended the server's competitive phase before it started. Alliances that didn't join the coalition were relegated to low-level areas and became de facto farm accounts for the ruling elite. By the time cross-world events unlocked, the server was filled with high-power players who had never truly fought for their territory, resulting in poor coordination and consistent losses.
Server: City Server 2045 (2023)
Summary: A council of the top 5 alliances established exclusive hunting grounds for high-level Raven units and bunkers, locking the rest of the server out from the best blueprints and components. They used a shared mapping tool to deconflict their farming routes, creating a resource monopoly that led to a massive power imbalance. During the first server-wide "Conquest" event, the server's forces were extremely top-heavy. The handful of super-powered players couldn't defend all fronts, and the vast majority of the server's under-equipped players were steamrolled. The swift defeat demoralized the population and solidified the server's reputation as a "whale's paradise" with no room for growth.
Server: Server 66 (2021)
Summary: A server-wide NAP was created with the sole purpose of maximizing performance in the "Contention of Ares" (SvS) event. The ruling council assigned cities and resource nodes based on spreadsheets tracking player power. Internal conflict was outlawed, and violators were publicly shamed and zeroed. The game became a bi-weekly cycle of preparing for SvS, with no room for internal politics or strategic warfare. Players who grew powerful enough to challenge the status quo were told to "wait their turn" for better territory, which could take months. This led to many leaving for younger servers where, as one player put it, there was "still a game to play between events."
System: Global (Ongoing)
Summary: This phenomenon is different as it's a voluntary, cross-server system involving thousands of clans. The Farm War Alliance (FWA) is an organization where member clans agree to set easy-to-beat "farm bases" during Clan Wars. They use a point system to manipulate matchmaking and ensure they frequently match with other FWA clans. One clan attacks with minimal troops to "lose," while the designated "winner" easily gets maximum stars, securing full war loot and Clan XP for both sides with little effort. While an efficient farming method, it completely circumvents the competitive nature of Clan Wars. Players who stay in this system for too long often find their attacking skills have atrophied, rendering them ineffective in genuine, competitive wars against non-FWA clans.

IV. The Cycle of Stagnation

This leads to the predictable Cycle of Stagnation:

  1. Phase 1 - Chaos: Server launch. Organic warfare. High activity.
  2. Phase 2 - Consolidation: Whales tire of risk. The NAP is formed.
  3. Phase 3 - Order: The NAP solidifies control. The pyramid structure is cemented.
  4. Phase 4 - Apathy: The lack of meaningful conflict leads to boredom. Player activity drops. The server's overall skill level decays.
  5. Phase 5 - Collapse: The server either dies a slow death from inactivity or is mercy-killed by a more aggressive, active server in a server-wide event, triggering a final exodus.

A NAP does not preserve a server. It embalms it.

V. Server Health Diagnosis: Symptoms of NAP Disease

Is your server infected? The symptoms are clinical and easily observable.

  • [ ] A Dead Global Chat: The primary sign of a sedated player base. When there is no conflict, there is nothing to talk about.
  • [ ] Scripted Events: Top alliances "take turns" winning events or agree on reward distribution beforehand. There is no real competition.
  • [ ] "No Kill" Rules: The NAP enforces complex rules about who can hit whom, when, and where. Hitting resource tiles may be forbidden. Attacking cities below a certain power level may be a "war crime." This is not peace; it is micromanagement.
  • [ ] Mass Migration of Mid-Core Players: The most ambitious F2P and low-spenders start leaving the server for younger, more chaotic kingdoms.
  • [ ] Rise of the "Diplomat" Player: The most influential figures on the server are not the best warriors, but the players who spend all day in Discord negotiating and managing the delicate NAP politics.
  • [ ] The Illusion of Choice: You are "free" to do anything, as long as it does not challenge the established order in any meaningful way.

If you can check three or more of these boxes, your server is in the advanced stages of NAP-induced decay.
